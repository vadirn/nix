Write for a reader who skims. Maximize clarity. Cut the rest.

## Where it applies

This style governs every piece of prose an agent writes:

- chat replies
- code comments
- commit messages
- PR bodies
- vault entries

The 20-word cap limits the sentence, not the comment. Split a long explanation into short sentences. Match the surrounding file's comment density and idiom, because "match the surrounding code" governs those, not sentence form. Apply the style only to a comment you write or edit.

The git skill and the vault skill hold the surface limits for commit subjects, PR bodies, and entry bodies. Follow those limits too, because a one-line subject or a fixed template bounds the prose this style shapes.

## Principles

Four principles from ISO 24495-1 order the rules below:

- Relevant — the reader gets what they need. See Relevance.
- Findable — the reader locates what they need. See Shape.
- Understandable — the reader grasps what they find. See Sentences and Words.
- Usable — the reader can act on what they find. See the step rules in Sentences and Shape.

## Relevance

- Cover what the reader needs. Cut what falls outside it.
- Use the fewest words that keep the meaning whole.
- Lead with the conclusion. Put the reason after it.

## Sentences

- Write one idea per sentence. Cap each sentence at 20 words.
- Split a sentence that carries two claims.
- Use active voice. Name the actor.
- Use the imperative for steps. Write "Run X," not "You should run X."
- Start with the known part. End with the new part.
- Keep the connective: because, so, but, although. Keep it even in a short sentence.

## Words

- Use one term per concept. Reuse that term.
- Prefer plain, concrete words. Cut any word the sentence survives without.
- Replace a hidden verb with a verb. Write "decide," not "make a decision."
- Use the positive form. State what to do.
- Use simple tenses: present, past, future.
- Use at most three nouns in a row. Split a longer noun stack.

## Shape

- Turn a sequence or a set into a vertical list.
- Give each paragraph one topic. Keep paragraphs short.
- Add a heading for each topic a reader may jump to.

## Relation to Exposition

The global instructions hold an Exposition section. It governs the argument: thesis, grounds, and derivation. This style governs the prose. They agree where they meet, at "lead with the conclusion" and "plain words." Follow both when both apply.

These rules adapt Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100) and ISO 24495-1:2023 (plain language). Apply only the rules above. Leave out STE's dictionary and its procedure-only rules.
